The Tribunale di Firenze (judge Umberto Castagnini) dismisses the request for authorisation to remain
of a Moroccan national who has been in Italy for about ten years, whose application the Commissione of
Florence — Livorno Section had rejected as manifestly unfounded in the accelerated procedure under
Article 42(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348. It is the first decision, among those collected, that
denies protection and takes a position on the limits of the principle laid down by the Joint Chambers
(Sezioni Unite) of the Court of Cassation, no. 11399/2024, under the new framework.

## Unsigned determination and plurality of grounds

The copy of the determination bore only “signed original on file”. Signature of the copy “does not, as
such, constitute an indispensable requirement of validity” where origin and content are not in doubt;
the information on the procedural choice, given on 13 July 2026 and repeated at the start of the
interview, fulfilled the function of a guarantee. As to the grounds, the Commissione did not confine
itself to the statistical criterion of point (*j*): Morocco is on the Union list of safe countries of
origin of Regulation (EU) 2026/464 (point (*e*)), and the applicant did not apply as soon as possible
in ten years of residence (point (*h*) or (*i*)). Concurrent grounds, all satisfied.

## The information duties are not a condition of the accelerated procedure

The information form was in Italian, untranslated. The Tribunale draws a distinction: the principle of
the Joint Chambers, no. 11399/2024 — where the accelerated procedure is breached, the ordinary one is
revived with automatic suspension — concerns “constitutive or in any event specific elements of the
accelerated procedure” (determination, time limits, conditions, including as regards the safe country:
CJEU *CV* and *Alace and Canpelli*). The duties under Article 8 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, by
contrast, are “a transversal procedural guarantee”, operating in every procedure; their breach does
not automatically revive the ordinary procedure, but “may justify authorisation to remain” where it
prevented the guarantees from being recovered: an unsuitable interview, or one held without the
applicant having been able to consult counsel. Here the interview took place with an interpreter and
with the counsel of his choice appointed from the time of the application, and nothing is alleged
about concrete prejudice; the guarantees of the Regulation “work together, according to an integrated
logic”, and informing about the procedure is among the duties of counsel. The decree draws the
criterion from CJEU, 30 November 2023, C-228/21 and others, and from Cass. no. 25310/2024 on the
Dublin leaflet: principles that cannot be transposed mechanically, but which express a general
principle of effectiveness. On the merits, the economic reasons for leaving and the integration
documented only for the period of detention do not establish the *fumus* of special protection.

The decision must be read alongside those pointing the other way on the information duties —
[Trib. Firenze 9251](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-firenze-9251-2026-08-06.html),
[Trib. Bologna 11342](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-11342-2026-07-21.html),
[Trib. Roma 39909](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-roma-39909-2026-09-12.html) — which treat them as a defect
of the accelerated procedure.
