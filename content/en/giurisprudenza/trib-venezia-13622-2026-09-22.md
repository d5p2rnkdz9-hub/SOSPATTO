The Tribunal of Venice (judge Alessia Capriuoli) records that the conditions for applying the
accelerated border procedure are not met, converts the procedure from Article
35-*ter* to Article 35-*bis* of Legislative Decree 25/2008, finds that the appellant has the right to remain in the
territory, orders the immediate issue of the document under Article 4(4) of Legislative Decree 142/2015 and
declares any imposing or restrictive measure automatically terminated. The decision of the Commissione territoriale of Padua, of 2 September 2026, had been taken in the border procedure
under Article 43(1), point (*a*), and Article 42(1), point (*j*), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, the applicant being
Tunisian.

## An information form neither signed nor timely undermines the procedure at the procedural level

The information form was not signed by the linguistic mediator and was drawn up on
26 August 2026, “following the date of submission and registration of the
application” (24 and 25 August respectively). The irregularity and lateness of the information (Article
8(2) and Article 15(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348) undermine the very formal correctness of the procedure, in
accordance with the principles, “exportable also to the current regime”, of the Joint Chambers (Sezioni Unite) no. 11399/2024:
all the information provided for in Article 8(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 must be provided “*as soon as possible* to
enable applicants to exercise the rights guaranteed by this Regulation”.

## Participatory guarantees and the 20% threshold

Statements made without adequate information are not “fully informed” and cannot
found the verification under Article 42(1), point (*j*), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, which requires comparison with the applicant's personal
history and not the statistical figure alone. The Tribunal places the violations within the
“constructive dialogue, based on loyalty and mutual trust” between the administration and the person concerned
(Article 4 TFEU; CJEU judgment of 14 May 2020, joined cases C-924/19 PPU and C-925/19 PPU).

## The relationship with the Ministerial Decree of 21 July 2026 on the authorised places

The procedure takes on “further hallmarks of unlawfulness”: the application was submitted on 24
August 2026, at a time when the Ministerial Decree of 21 July 2026
identifying the places authorised to carry out border procedures had not yet entered into force.
Referring to Court of Cassation no. 18531 of 18 May 2026, the Tribunal notes that in intertemporal assessments “primary
weight” is to be given “to the original phase of expression of the wish to apply”, since from that point the
Authorities are under an obligation to “channel” the application into the procedure provided for. The notification under Article 54 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, to which the Ministerial Decree is linked (Article 2(2)
of the decree), is not of merely formal value but “rests on a careful and considered
assessment of the places equipped with all the adequate facilities and competences”: the transfer of
persons to places not designated under Article 54 — “the only ones endowed with the so-called legal fiction of
non-entry” — before the entry into force of the Ministerial Decree “automatically gives rise to full
authorisation to enter the national territory”, a fact incompatible with the border
procedure.
