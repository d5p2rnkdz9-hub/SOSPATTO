The Tribunal of Florence (judge Umberto Castagnini) suspends the measure of the
Commissione territoriale of Florence of 20 July 2026 — notified on 3 September and challenged on
10 September — and authorises the appellant to remain in the national territory until the
proceedings are concluded. The decree is decided on the “most liquid ground”: the failure to file
the Commission's defence notes within the time limit of Article 35-*bis*(4) of Legislative Decree 25/2008
places on the administration the burden of documenting actual compliance with the procedural
obligations.

## Screening and the procedure: two autonomous items of information

The core is the non-interchangeability of the information under Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1356 and that under
Article 8 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348. The former “aims to enable the competent authorities to establish
whether the person concerned is to be referred to the international protection procedure or to
return procedures” and serves the preliminary checks (security, health,
vulnerability). The latter, “at the latest at the time of registration of the application”, ensures
“the effective participation of the applicant in the administrative procedure”: stages, time limits,
rights and obligations, consequences of failure to cooperate, means of submitting
elements, the right to free legal counselling and to assistance. Recital 16
stresses the provision of “complete information” at a “very early” stage.

## Free legal counselling and the effectiveness of the information

Legal counselling under Articles 15 and 16 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 is provided “on request”, but the
authorities have an autonomous obligation to inform the applicant of the existence of that right, “and,
in any event, no later than the time of registration of the application”. The effectiveness of the right
presupposes “prior knowledge of its existence and of the means of access”:
information is not “an accessory element” but “a guarantee functional to the effective exercise
of the rights of defence”. The mere information under Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1356 does not make up for it.

## The remedy: authorisation to remain, not automatic reinstatement

Breach of the information duty under Article 8 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 “although it does not automatically entail the reinstatement of the ordinary procedure and of the consequent suspensive effect *ex
lege*”, is relevant to verifying the effectiveness of the participatory guarantees and supports the request
for authorisation to remain under Article 68(4) of the Regulation and Article 35-*bis*(4) of Legislative Decree 25/2008.
Article 47 of the EU Charter “requires that a remedy be ensured that is capable of recovering in judicial proceedings
the guarantee not fully ensured at the administrative stage”. The reference to the CJEU cases
*Alace and Canpelli* (C-758/24 and C-759/24) reinforces the extension of judicial review to the
manner in which the administrative procedure was conducted.

Castagnini confirms — as against
[Trib. Firenze 11088](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-firenze-11088-2026-09-11.html) — the middle course: the
information breach does not reinstate the ordinary procedure *ex lege*, but justifies
authorisation to remain; the novelty lies in the analytical distinction between the screening information and the
procedure information, which closes the door on the substitutability of the two.
