The Tribunale di Napoli (judge Dr Mario Suriano) raised of its own motion the question of the intertemporal
regime and declared the contested decision suspended *ex lege* by reason of the bringing of the appeal.
The proceedings concerned the refusal as manifestly unfounded adopted by the Commissione territoriale of
Caserta pursuant to Article 28-*ter* of Legislative Decree 25/2008 and Article 39(4) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348;
the appellant's grounds are declared absorbed, because upstream there is a question "of law derivable
from the records and logically preliminary".

## The gap in Article 79

Article 79 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 refers the new regime to applications **lodged** from 12 June 2026, but "does not expressly
govern the hypothesis […] in which the wish to apply for protection was expressed before 12 June 2026
and the lodging took place only afterwards".

The reasoning rests on Article 3(1), point 12, which defines the "application for international
protection" as the expression of the wish to apply for protection: the provision "attributes autonomous
legal relevance to the expression of the wish, identifying that moment as the birth of the application,
while the subsequent lodging constitutes a distinct procedural stage". The decisive step is functional:

> "This normative silence cannot be interpreted as attributing exclusive relevance to the moment of
> lodging, since such a reading would allow the Administration, through the mere deferral of the
> lodging activity, to affect unilaterally the identification of the applicable legal regime, to the
> detriment of the principles of legal certainty, protection of legitimate expectations, equal treatment
> and effectiveness of judicial protection".

A systemic argument is added: giving exclusive weight to lodging would neutralise the definition of
Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, "intended to operate for the entire Regulation", precisely with regard to the
transitional rules, "without the European legislature having expressed a derogating intention in
unequivocal terms". Confirmation is drawn from Article 17 of Decree-Law 100/2026, which "maintains the
application of the former rules to applications previously made, confirming the relevance of the
genetic moment of the application".

In the case at hand it emerged from documents, from a certified e-mail (PEC) produced in the proceedings, that the wish had been
expressed on 15 December 2025 and the lodging had taken place only on 25 June 2026.

## The effect

Once the former rules are applied, the use of the accelerated procedure is unlawful and "brings the
procedure back within the ordinary rules, with the consequent automatic production, by operation of
law, of the suspensive effect deriving from the bringing of the judicial appeal". The decree does not
grant discretionary suspensive effect: it finds that suspension operates *ex lege*.

In the same sense [Trib. Bologna, 12 August 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-12435-2026-08-12.html);
for the opposite position on the literal wording of Article 79,
[Trib. Firenze, 24 August 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-firenze-11120-2026-08-24.html); for the reverse
case — former rules applied to an application lodged after 12 June —
[Trib. Torino, 11 August 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-torino-2026-08-11.html).
