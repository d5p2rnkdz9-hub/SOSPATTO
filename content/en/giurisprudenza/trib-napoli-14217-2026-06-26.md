Two weeks after the entry into force of Decree-Law 100/2026, the Tribunal of Naples (judge Cristina Correale)
refuses to validate a measure alternative to detention applied to an applicant in the border procedure: in that procedure the first measure is the authorisation to
reside in a specific place, which the new Article 5-*ter*(2) of Legislative Decree 142/2015 requires to be “always”
adopted, and the alternative measures are alternatives *to detention*, not to that authorisation.

## The facts

An Algerian national, who arrived in Italy on 1 April 2026, submitted an application for protection to the Questura
of Naples “on an unspecified date”; the application is examined under the border procedure pursuant to Article 43 of Regulation (EU)
2024/1348. By decree of 23 June 2026 the Questore ordered, under Articles 6 and 6-*bis*(2)
of Legislative Decree 142/2015 and Article 14(1-*bis*)(*c*) of Legislative Decree 286/1998 (Consolidated Immigration Act), the obligation to report
weekly to the Questura, giving as grounds the risk of absconding (Article 5-*sexies*, points (*a*) and (*b*) of Legislative Decree 142/2015).
The Administration filed nothing beyond the decree.

## The burden of proving that the application falls within the new regime

The Tribunal starts from a “preliminary and absorbing” finding: the Questore applied rules introduced by Decree-Law 100/2026, in force from 12 June, but the decree does not state the date of the application and the public administration
did not file the registration. There is therefore no proof that the application falls within the new regime.

## The sequence under Article 5-*ter*

Article 5-*ter*(2) of Legislative Decree 142/2015 provides that where the application is examined pursuant to Article 43 of the Regulation
“the authorisation referred to in paragraph 1 is always adopted […], unless the conditions for ordering
detention or a measure alternative to detention are met”. The judge derives a ladder from it:

> It follows from the wording of the provision that the application of the alternative measure presupposes that upstream
> the authorisation to reside in a specific place has been adopted […] and that, where the conditions are met, an alternative measure to detention may then also be applied — evidently in compliance with the principle of proportionality and gradualness of the
> measures — where the authorisation alone referred to in paragraph 1 does not satisfy the needs of the specific case.

Those measures “are alternatives to detention but not to the authorisation to reside in a specific
place”. In the present case the authorisation was never issued, so that the alternative measure
is left without a basis: not validated.

The reading is not the only possible one — the clause “unless the conditions are met” could be read
as an exception to the obligation to authorise — but Naples chooses the gradualist one and anchors it to the principle
of proportionality. It follows that the Questore cannot skip the step of the Prefetto, to whom Article
5-*quater* of Legislative Decree 142/2015 entrusts the authorisation, and build on his own the regime of control of the applicant at the
border.

On the prohibition of turning the authorisation to reside into disguised detention see
[Trib. Trieste, 7 August 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-trieste-4412-2026-08-07.html) and
[Trib. Palermo, R.G. 8694](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-palermo-8694-2026-07-25.html); on jurisdiction for
validation, [Trib. Bologna, 22 June 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-9726-2026-06-22.html).
