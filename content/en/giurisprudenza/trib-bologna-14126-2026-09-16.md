The Tribunal of Bologna (judge Luca Minniti) finds that the appeal has suspended the enforceability
of the rejection as manifestly unfounded and declares that there is no need to rule on the request for
interim relief. The appellant had expressed the wish to apply on 12 March 2026; the application was
lodged with form C3 on 6 July 2026, according to the schedule set by the administration,
and the Commission applied Regulation (EU) 2024/1348.

## The criterion of Article 79(3) and the conflict with Article 17(4) of Decree-Law 100/2026

The decree starts from the “unequivocal wording”: “lodged” (*introduites* in the French version) refers to
the stage of Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, distinct from the application made (Article 26) and registered (Article 27).
The Regulation therefore applies to all applications lodged from 12 June 2026, “irrespective of the
time at which the applicant expressed his or her wish”. Article 17(4) of Decree-Law 100/2026
“flagrantly conflicts” with that provision, and the national rule must be read in the light of
the EU one, not the other way round.

The judge shares neither the reading of
[Trib. Napoli 17179-1/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-napoli-17179-2026-08-04.html), according to which Article 79(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348
would not govern an application submitted before and lodged after 12 June, nor that of the
decree of the same Tribunal of 15 September 2026 (R.G. 12956-1/2026), which requires a lodging that complies with the Regulation: the transitional
rule refers to “a historical fact”, and defects in the lodging affect the procedural consequences, not the regime
applicable.

## The protection of legitimate expectations

The corrective lies elsewhere. Following
[Trib. Bologna 12908-1/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-12908-2026-08-17.html) and
[Trib. Firenze 11120/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-firenze-11120-2026-08-24.html), the decree
holds that the effects of Article 79(3) must be tempered “not to modify the criterion laid down
for the transitional regime but only to deprive the rule of its abnormal prejudicial effects”, where
they depend on the timetable unilaterally set by the administration. The applicant
could rely on the application being lodged within the previous nine-day time limit (Article 28-*bis*(2)
of Legislative Decree 25/2008): had it been complied with, the application would have remained subject to the
previous rules.

Since that time limit had already expired on 12 June, the principle of Court of Cassation, Joint Chambers (Sezioni Unite), no.
11399/2024 applies: the derogation from automatic suspensive effect presupposes the correct and timely
conduct of the accelerated procedure.

On the premise, the decree conflicts with
[Trib. Bologna 13060-1/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-13060-2026-09-20.html), which holds
Article 17(4) of Decree-Law 100/2026 to be compatible with the Regulation; the outcome is the same.
