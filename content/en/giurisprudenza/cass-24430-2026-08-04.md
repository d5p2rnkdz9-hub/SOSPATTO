The First Civil Section of the Court of Cassation (president Maria Acierno, rapporteur Martina Flamini) rejects
the case law of the criminal sections on the immediate removability of an applicant whose application has
been rejected as manifestly unfounded or inadmissible. The appeal was against decree no. 21788/2026 of 22 June 2026
of the Justice of the Peace of Turin. Decree-Law 100/2026 returned the subject of detention to the civil
sections, after eighteen months in which it had fallen within the jurisdiction of the criminal sections.

## The obligation to leave the territory arises on expiry of the time limit for appeal

The Court starts from Article 32(4) of Legislative Decree 25/2008:

> “The applicant's obligation to leave the national territory, in the cases indicated above [...] arises
> **only on expiry of the time limit for appeal**.”

The rule applies to all the cases listed in the provision: simple rejection (point (*b*)), rejection as
manifestly unfounded (point (*b-bis*)), internal protection alternative (point (*b-ter*)),
inadmissibility in the cases of Article 38(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 (point (*b-quater*)), in addition to the situations
covered by Articles 23, 23-*bis*, 29 and 29-*bis* of Legislative Decree 25/2008. Until that time limit expires, the certificate of the
obligation to return, although it takes the place of the expulsion order, cannot be enforced.

The case concerned a first subsequent application declared inadmissible for lack of novelty. The
Court places the situation precisely: it is “neither a second or further
subsequent application, nor an application made solely to delay or frustrate the enforcement of a return
or removal decision”, so that it falls under Article 29 of Legislative Decree 25/2008 — which refers to Article 38(2)
of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 — and not under Articles 56 and 68(6) of that Regulation, the only provisions that
would allow the right to remain to be excluded. The other cases of inadmissibility in Article 38(1)
are instead referred to by the new Article 32(1)(*b-quater*) of Legislative Decree 25/2008.

Even under the post-Pact rules, therefore, the applicant cannot be expelled until the time limit for appeal has expired
without an appeal being brought, irrespective of the type of rejection decision. Since the detention
had been ordered on 20 June 2026 and the inadmissibility decision was dated 15 June, the time limit had not
expired: the decision “could not be enforced, with the consequence that the detention
ordered was unlawful”.

## The dissent from the criminal sections

The Court takes a position on Court of Cassation, Criminal Division, Section I, no. 37701 of 12 November 2025 (which referred to Court of Cassation,
Criminal Division, Section I, no. 22275 of 12 June 2025), according to which, in the case of Article 35-*bis*(3)(*b*) of Legislative Decree 25/2008,
the inadmissibility decision is immediately enforceable: “that conclusion cannot be
endorsed”, also because of the “changed regulatory environment” compared with the one examined in those
rulings. The coordination with Article 35-*bis* is drawn by distinguishing the two levels: Article 32(4)
governs the right to await the expiry of the time limit before the obligation to leave the
territory arises, whereas Article 35-*bis* governs the right to remain in the phase following the lodging of the appeal.

## The scope of review by the validating judge

The defect of the Turin decree is traced back to the extent of the review that the Constitution requires of the validating
judge. The Court refers to Constitutional Court no. 203/2024 (detention in the CPR affects personal
liberty) and to the “full meaning” of review affirmed by Constitutional Court no. 105/2001, which “cannot stop
at the margins of the expulsion procedure”, and reaffirms its own case law (Court of Cassation no. 16496/2024,
no. 30166/2023, no. 18404/2023): the verification is not limited to the conditions of Articles 13(4-*bis*) and
14(1) of Legislative Decree 286/1998, but extends to “the existence and effectiveness of the expulsion measure” and
to its “manifest unlawfulness”, an indispensable precondition for the deprivation of liberty. The Justice
of the Peace should therefore have assessed whether, at the time of the detention, the time limit for appeal had
expired.

## Jurisdiction remains with the Justice of the Peace

The second ground, examined first because it concerns jurisdiction, is instead rejected. The
appellant argued that, while the time limit for appealing the Commission's decision was still running, validation
belonged to the Tribunal (Articles 2 and 6 of Legislative Decree 142/2015). The Court replies that the certificate of the
obligation to return under Article 32(4) of Legislative Decree 25/2008 “takes the place of and produces the effects of” the
expulsion measure, and that the Questore acts under Article 13(4) and (5) of Legislative Decree 286/1998;
paragraph 5.2 of the same Article 13 vests jurisdiction in the Justice of the Peace. The detention had
been ordered “in the phase of enforcement of the expulsion”: this is not detention under
Article 6 of Legislative Decree 142/2015, so that the principles on the jurisdiction of the Tribunal based on the
continuation of *applicant* status until the final decision do not apply.

The criterion cannot be entirely superimposed on that of the twin order
[no. 24431/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/cass-24431-2026-08-04.html), which gives weight to the status of
applicant “irrespective of the legal basis on which the detention was ordered”. The point of
connection lies in the facts: there the detainee had renewed the expression of the wish to apply before the
Questura, here not. Pre-removal detention of a person who has received the certificate under Article 32(4)
therefore remains before the Justice of the Peace, who must however verify that it is enforceable.

## The new procedure for appeals to the Court of Cassation (Article 14.1 of Legislative Decree 286/1998)

This is one of the first applications of the procedure introduced by Article 12(2)(*g*) of Decree-Law 100/2026. The
Court lays down the intertemporal rule: the new regime applies to measures on detention,
extensions and reviews “issued by the competent judicial body from 12 June 2026”, the date of entry into
force of the decree (Article 19 of Decree-Law 100/2026). Since this concerns the rules governing the form of appeal, the date of the contested measure
counts and not that on which the application was lodged. The appeal had been
brought according to the former model, but the divergence does not affect its admissibility, since service on the
Avvocatura generale and registration of the case were timely.

The order reconstructs the passage from the old Article 14(6) of Legislative Decree 286/1998 (appeal within
five days, only on the grounds of Article 606(1)(*a*), (*b*) and (*c*) of the Code of Criminal Procedure), repealed by
point (*f*) of the same Article 12 of Decree-Law 100/2026, to the new Article 14.1 of Legislative Decree 286/1998:

- appeal to the Court of Cassation under Article 360 of the Code of Civil Procedure within **ten days** of the pronouncement at the hearing or
  of the communication, against the decree on validation, extension or review of detention or of the
  alternative measures;
- filing **on pain of inadmissibility within five days** of service (Article 369,
  second paragraph, of the Code of Civil Procedure applies);
- concise defence brief of the other party within five days of filing, which may also contain grounds
  for a cross-appeal;
- decision in chambers **within thirty days** of filing, with notice to the lawyers and
  the public prosecutor at least fifteen days before the sitting; written submissions of the public prosecutor up to ten
  days before, documents under Article 372 of the Code of Civil Procedure within the same time limit, explanatory briefs up to five days
  before; sitting also possible remotely;
- briefly reasoned order filed at the end of the deliberation in chambers, or only the operative part with
  reasons within the following fifteen days; for the rest, the Code of Civil Procedure
  to the extent compatible.

## The fate of the Questore's measure

The panel also restates the rule that “the quashing of the validation decree deprives the administrative measure
of effect *ex tunc*”, since this is the exercise of an exceptional authoritative power that is rigidly regulated also as regards its
temporal effectiveness: hence the decision on the merits, without remand. The operative part quashes the decree of the Justice of the Peace, “does not validate the
detention” ordered by the Questore of Turin on 20 June 2026 “and declares it ineffective”: detention
ordered before the expiry of the time limit for appeal never had a valid
basis. As to costs, the detainee is admitted to legal aid at the State's expense *ex lege* and the lawyer's fee is to be
determined under Articles 82 and 83 of Presidential Decree 115/2002, with no order on costs before the Court of Cassation.

The same order of verification — first the enforceability of the obligation to return, then detention or
alternative measures — was applied on the same date by the Justice of the Peace of Trieste, R.G. 1738/2026, covered in
the summary
[Trib. Trieste, R.G. 4385/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-trieste-4385-2026-08-04.html). To be read together with the twin
order [no. 24431/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/cass-24431-2026-08-04.html) on jurisdiction for
validation.
