The Tribunale di Roma (judge Francesco Rossini) suspended the enforceability of the decision by which
the Ministry of the Interior — Dublin Unit had ordered the applicant’s transfer to Belgium. It is the
first decision published here on the transitional regime of Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 (AMMR), which
replaces Dublin III.

## The rules applicable to an application registered before 1 July 2026

The contested decision records that the applicant “registered an application for international
protection in Italy on 30 April 2026”. The Tribunale cites in full Article 84 of Regulation (EU)
2024/1351, paragraph 2 of which provides that “for applications for international protection
registered before 1 July 2026 the Member State responsible for examination is determined in
accordance with the criteria set out in Regulation (EU) No 604/2013”. The registration precedes the
threshold and therefore falls within paragraph 2.

The decree does not, however, state the principle — still disputed — whether the reference in
paragraph 2 to the “criteria” of responsibility alone draws in the whole of Regulation (EU) No 604/2013
(time limits for take back, information duties, interview) or applies to the criteria in the strict
sense: on that point it takes no position.

## The grounds for suspension

The Tribunale assesses “overall” two elements, holding them sufficient to make up the “serious and
detailed grounds” of Article 3(3-*quater*) of Legislative Decree 25/2008.

**The Dublin Unit’s decision is incomplete.** The transfer decree gives reasons by referring to the
note of 25.06.2026 by which Belgium acknowledged its responsibility, but reads:

> …since the conditions laid down in Article **Choose an item**. of Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 are met
> in the present case…

“Choose an item” is the placeholder text of a drop-down menu left unfilled: the act does not indicate
which article of the Regulation establishes responsibility. The Tribunale notes that the reference to
the Belgian note “does not appear formally complete”, but the consequence is substantive — a decision
ordering transfer to another Member State does not say on the basis of which provision. Since it is a
standardised template, the point goes beyond the individual case.

**The reference to private and social life is vague**, being disposed of in standard terms “in the
face of the specific allegations and documents provided in the present proceedings”. According to
the defence, the applicant is expecting a child with his partner: a circumstance not considered by the
administrative decision and not apparent from the text of the order.

The Tribunale then considers appearance unnecessary under Article 3(3-*septies*) of Legislative
Decree 25/2008, deals with the case on the papers and orders the applicant to document any presence in
a reception facility at the time the appeal was filed.

## Questions the decree does not address

It is not clarified which domestic provision sets the time limit for appealing, given that Article
43(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 merely requires States to provide a time limit “of at least one
week but not more than three weeks”: in the absence of case law, the prevailing reading is that the
thirty-day limit of Article 3(3-*ter*) of Legislative Decree 25/2008 remains, which no domestic
provision has adapted — a limit which, however, exceeds the three-week ceiling. It also remains to be
determined whether, for appeals subject to the new Regulation, the typification of grounds in Article
43 still allows failings in the information duties and in the interview to be pleaded.
