The Tribunal of Florence (judge Umberto Castagnini) suspends the decision declaring a subsequent application
inadmissible from a position that is original compared with the other decisions in this line:
the regulation **applies** to an application lodged after 12 June 2026, because the wording of Article
79(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 «does not appear capable of being overridden by the interpreter»; but the Administration's delay cannot be
held against the applicant.

The applicant had expressed the wish to submit a new application on 8 May 2026 and was able to
lodge it only on 30 July, «according to the timetable set by the
administrative authority».

## The interim jurisdiction of the court seised

The decision comes from a section of the Commission based in Perugia, so that «on a first
assessment» jurisdiction over the merits would lie with the Tribunal of Perugia. The Florence judge does not
decline: applying the principle of Article 669-*quater* of the Code of Civil Procedure, and because removal carried out
before the question of jurisdiction is settled «could produce effects that are hardly
reversible», interim protection «cannot be deferred until the final identification of the
competent court». The measure remains «strictly interim and protective».

## The wording of Article 79(3)

«Lodging» has a technical meaning in the regulation, which distinguishes the application *made* (Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348),
*registered* (Article 27) and *lodged* (Article 28); Article 79(3) chooses the third. Having acknowledged the
force of the opposing argument drawn from recital 27, the judge considers that the opposite
interpretative option «meets a not insignificant obstacle in the wording»: this is the position
contrary to that of [Trib. Naples](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-napoli-17179-2026-08-04.html).

## The delay cannot be held against the applicant

The decree distinguishes «two logically independent problems»: which rules apply, and whether the public administration's delay
may produce unfavourable consequences for a person who did in good time what could be expected of him. To
the second question it answers that

> it is an expression of a general principle of coherence of the legal system that
> the authority cannot benefit from its own failure to comply with an obligation, nor derive from it
> unfavourable consequences for a person who has promptly exercised his right

The applicant could rely on compliance with the time limits then in force (Article 26(2-*bis*) of Legislative Decree
25/2008: three days, extendable to ten); otherwise «the breach of the time limit binding the authority
would be treated as a condition for applying more restrictive rules».

## The five conditions

So that the criterion does not empty Article 79 of substance, the Tribunal confines it to five conditions: expression
of the wish prior to 12 June 2026; addressed to an authority competent to receive it or required to forward
it without delay; cooperation by the applicant, who must not have contributed to the postponement;
expiry, already by that date, of the former time limit, taking extensions into account — «and therefore, as a general rule, with reference to expressions of the wish prior to 1 June 2026», which excludes a person who
expressed the wish between 2 and 11 June; a concretely unfavourable consequence arising from the
new regime. Here the time limits had long expired, so that «the unfavourable consequence arising from the special
characterisation of the procedure cannot be held against the
applicant for the purposes of the right to remain».

The result coincides, by a different route, with that of
[Trib. Turin, 24 August 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-torino-16082-2026-08-24.html) and
[Trib. Bologna, 12 August 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-12435-2026-08-12.html); the requirement
that the delay be attributable to the Administration is shared by
[Trib. Bologna, 4 September 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-13590-2026-09-04.html).
