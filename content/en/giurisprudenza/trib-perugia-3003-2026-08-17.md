The Tribunal of Perugia (judge Elena Stramaccioni) suspended the enforceability of the
decision by which the *Commissione territoriale* of Florence — Perugia Section had declared
the application manifestly unfounded. The transitional regime is here decided not on the date of the application, but on
the rules that the Commission actually applied.

## The applicable rules

The application was made on 1 July 2026, hence after the entry into force of Decree-Law 100/2026; the
Commission nonetheless decided on 9 July declaring it manifestly unfounded under Article
28-*ter*(1)(a) of Legislative Decree 25/2008 «in the wording prior to that introduced by the
Decree-Law cited». According to the defence, which framed the appeal under both regimes, this
would not be an isolated case.

The Tribunal starts from Article 17(4) of Decree-Law 100/2026, notes the discrepancy and concludes:

> it is therefore held that the examination of the appeal against the decision of the Commission and of the accompanying interim
> application must necessarily be conducted applying the former legislation and that
> therefore, as a preliminary matter, **the appeal must be regarded as filed in time**.

The last clause is the practical reason why the question matters: the implicit premise is that the two regimes
carry different time limits for appeal, so that a person who followed the former rules — as
the Commission had done — would risk being time-barred if the court applied the new ones. ⚠️ *The
comparison between the time limits is not made in the decision: it is an inference from the text, to be verified before
relying on it in a pleading.*

In the same direction is the finding that, as to the regularity of the accelerated procedure of Article
28-*bis*(2) of Legislative Decree 25/2008, «compliance with the **overall time limit of 9 days**, given the invariance
of the interests at stake» is sufficient — complied with in this case. This reading looks to the final time limit rather than to the internal sequence
of the stages, and diverges from
[Trib. Bologna, 4 September 2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-13590-2026-09-04.html), according to which all the stages of the
procedure must be complied with.

The criterion is also distinct from that of
[Trib. Turin, 11 August](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-torino-2026-08-11.html), which starts from the same anomaly —
the administration applying repealed rules to an application made after 12 June — but treats it as a defect
of the act, whereas here the administrative choice becomes the rule of decision; and from
[Trib. Naples, 4 August](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-napoli-17179-2026-08-04.html), which decides on the date of the
expression of the wish. The question whether the Commission may «choose» the applicable
legislation remains open.

## The periculum: continuity of medical investigations

The Tribunal sets aside the aspect of socio-occupational integration, documented only by the report of the
foundation hosting the applicant, and gives weight to the medical situation: a radiology report of 23
July 2026 shows enlarged lymph nodes in several sites, for which «clinical and laboratory work-up and subsequent specialist assessment are
necessary» is prescribed. Refusal of the application

> would expose the applicant to the danger of **interruption of medical investigations**, the results of which
> must in any case be produced in the proceedings on the merits, also in order to assess, of the court's own motion,
> whether the conditions of Article 19(2)(d-*bis*) of Legislative Decree 286/1998
> for the issue of the permit for medical treatment are met.

Suspensive effect is therefore granted so as not to interrupt a diagnostic investigation still under way, without
waiting for a definitive diagnosis; and the judge reserves the ex officio assessment of the permit for medical
treatment, although the appeal sought only special protection (*protezione speciale*).
