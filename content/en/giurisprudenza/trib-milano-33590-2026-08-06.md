The Tribunal of Milan (judge Elena Masetti Zannini) does not validate the obligation to report
weekly to the Questura imposed by the Questore of Monza and Brianza, on 4 August 2026, on an
applicant released from the CPR, under Article 6(2)(*b*) and (*d*) and Article 6-*quater* of Legislative Decree 142/2015. The
proceedings were conducted in written form under Article 14(1-*bis*) of Legislative Decree 286/1998 (Consolidated Immigration Act) (Constitutional Court
no. 280/2019; Court of Cassation no. 18766/2026), with the appointment of a court-appointed defence lawyer; no brief was
filed. The decree complements
[Trib. Milano 33108](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-milano-33108-2026-08-03.html) on the premise — the alternative
measure requires, upstream, the grounds for detention — shifting the emphasis to the burden of proof.

## The burden of proof lies on the Administration

The request for validation “initiates civil judicial proceedings based […] on the civil procedural rules
on the burden of proof”: it is for the Authority to prove the elements on which the
detention and the measure are based. The Questura produced only the documentation of the protection
procedure — certificate of expression of the wish to apply, an extract, partly illegible, of the Commission of Monza's determination under Article 42(1), point (*f*), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, information sheet, form on
voluntary departure, information record — “from which no element of the alleged habitual
commission of offences can be inferred”; neither fingerprint records nor criminal record.

## Dangerousness is not habitual offending

Point (*b*) refers to Article 13(2)(*c*) of Legislative Decree 286/1998 and to the categories of Article 1 of Legislative Decree
159/2011, which require “factual elements” (Court of Cassation no. 26173/2023; Court of Cassation no. 10022/2026 on the Justice
of the Peace who merely refers to precedents). The criminal record obtained by the court of its own motion shows two convictions
for robbery and attempted robbery, the second committed five months after the first sentence was served: “a dangerousness
to public order and security therefore emerges”, but “the prerequisite of the provision at issue, namely
habitual offending, is lacking”, which presupposes an assessment by the criminal court (Articles 102 and 103 of the Criminal Code)
that never took place. It also notes that the person concerned, who entered in 2015 as an unaccompanied minor, has been
in Italy for about eleven years without any element having been produced for a global assessment of his
personality.

## Indicators of absconding must be documented

On point (*d*): the false declaration of identity is unsupported; non-compliance with a
measure is neither indicated nor proved; the lack of a passport and the evasion of checks are *in re
ipsa* for someone who arrived from the Sicilian coast, but it is not known whether he was received as a minor. Decisive,
again, is the failure to invoke the first prerequisite — the elements of the application to be obtained —
with reference to Court of Cassation no. 21523/2026.
