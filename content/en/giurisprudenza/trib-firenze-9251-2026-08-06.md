The Tribunale di Firenze (judge Francesco Manfredi) authorised the applicant to remain “for the
whole duration of the proceedings until the decision on the merits”. The applicant is a national of
Bangladesh who disembarked at the port of Livorno on 14 June 2026 — two days after the entry into
force of Decree-Law 100/2026 — and was then transferred to a PAF in the province of Siena; the
rejection was for manifest unfoundedness under Article 39 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, following the
accelerated procedure of Article 42(1), points (*e*) and (*j*), of that Regulation, both of which had
been invoked.

## The duty to examine the request

The judge notes that Article 68(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 excludes the right to remain where the
rejection for manifest unfoundedness follows an accelerated procedure, and that Article 35-*bis*(4)
and (4-*bis*) of Legislative Decree 25/2008 nevertheless requires the court to examine the factual
and legal elements set out in the request: “since the applicant has made a timely request for
authorisation to remain, **it is necessary to assess that request**”.

## The guarantees of Chapter II apply in the accelerated procedure too

Article 42(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 requires the determining authority to respect the basic
principles and guarantees of Chapter II of that Regulation. Article 8(2) lists what the applicant must
be informed of in a language he understands: the stages and time limits of the procedure, rights and
obligations and the consequences of failing to comply with them — “in particular as regards explicit
or implicit withdrawal” of the application —, the right to free legal counselling and to legal
assistance at all stages, and the means of discharging the burden of submitting the elements
supporting the application. The duty “is timely and preventive and, in particular, functional […] to
enable the applicant for protection to exercise the rights guaranteed by the EU regulation and to
discharge his obligations adequately”, and must be read in the light of recital 31.

The finding of fact is the most significant step:

> “there is no exhaustive documentation drafted in a language the applicant for protection can
> understand […]; indeed, **the only documents handed to the applicant are those provided for the
> *screening* procedure** (see Article 11 of Regulation (EU) No 1356/2024), moreover those documents
> are also **drafted in Italian**, apart from a brief summary *screening* form in two languages”.

The screening information sheets are therefore not those of the asylum procedure: Regulation (EU)
2024/1356 and Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 impose distinct information duties, and discharging the
former does not discharge the latter. In the same sense, on the reference to a form with a link or QR
code, [Trib. Palermo, R.G. 8695/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-palermo-8695-2026-07-25.html).

Procedurally, “the Commissione has not documented in the present proceedings the fulfilment of the
duties within the time limits laid down by Article 35-*bis*(4) of Legislative Decree 25/2008, as
amended”: the burden lies on the Administration. The breach, “having prevented the applicant for
protection from the adequate, informed and timely exercise of the rights provided for by the EU
regulation (including that to legal counselling)”, is held to absorb every other question “by virtue
of the more straightforward ground”.

## The scope of the decision

The breaches are assessed only for the purposes of authorisation to remain, and not as defects
affecting the procedure for examining the application: the decree does not recharacterise the
procedure as ordinary. This is the difference from the Trieste line, where the defect in the border
procedure leads to [converting the procedure](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-trieste-4412-2026-08-07.html),
and from [Naples](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-napoli-17179-2026-08-04.html), where the error as to the
applicable regime causes automatic suspension *ex lege* to revive.
