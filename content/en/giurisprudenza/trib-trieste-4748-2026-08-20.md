The Tribunale di Trieste (judge Michela Bortolami) found that the conditions for applying the
accelerated border procedure were not met, converted the procedure from Article 35-*ter* to Article
35-*bis* of Legislative Decree 25/2008 and declared the contested decision automatically suspended
for the whole duration of the proceedings. It is the first decision given **after** the publication in
the Official Gazette of the [Ministerial Decree of 21 July 2026](/en/circolari/dm-zone-frontiera-2026-07-21.html),
on 10 August, whereas the Trieste decrees of [4](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-trieste-4385-2026-08-04.html),
[7](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-trieste-4412-2026-08-07.html) and
[8 August](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-trieste-4538-2026-08-08.html) could still confine themselves to
noting that the ministerial decree had not been adopted.

## The relevant moment is the start of the procedure

The Tribunale reiterates that the source required by Article 28-*bis* “has not yet been adopted”,
specifying the relevant moment — “at the time the procedure in question was started” — and
acknowledging that the Ministerial Decree was published on 10 August 2026. The Commissione had decided
on 29 July and the appeal was filed on 3 August; the judge decides on 20 August, with the Ministerial
Decree already in force, and nevertheless disapplies the procedure. The point is also stated
positively: the need for a clear delineation of the places

> required the public administration to await the adoption and entry into force of a new decree of the
> Ministry of the Interior in order to apply the procedures in question in the territory of Trieste and
> Gorizia.

The supervening publication therefore does not cure procedures started before 10 August.

## Why the Ministerial Decree of 5 August 2019 does not fill the gap

The Commissione had relied on Article 2(1), point (*a*), of the Ministerial Decree of 5 August 2019,
which lists among the border or transit zones those “existing” in the provinces of Trieste and
Gorizia. That decree, however, implemented Article 28-*bis*(1-*quater*) of Legislative Decree 25/2008
“in force at the time and **now repealed by Decree-Law 100/2026**”, served to extend the *administrative*
accelerated procedure to those who had evaded border controls, and refers to directives now repealed.

## The delineation of border and transit zones

The difficulty arises upstream: in the new legal framework there is no “clear definition of *border
zone* and, above all, of *transit zone*”, the latter concept being “far more nebulous”. Precisely
because the application of procedures affecting freedom of movement depends on them, they “require […]
a clear delineation at least at the time of adopting the domestic legal instruments”. The 2019
Ministerial Decree “assimilates and confuses” the two concepts and does not clarify “whether the cited
territories contain an *external border crossing point* and which one it is”: the Commissione’s thesis
that the whole territory of the two provinces is relevant therefore cannot be accepted. That vagueness
“could be explained” as long as the substantive condition was the evasion of controls; once that
filter has fallen, it “has by now lost its descriptive scope”.

## The procedural effect

The proceedings continue in the forms of Article 35-*bis* “and not Article 35-*ter* […], observed
until now”. Among the exceptions to the suspensive effect in paragraph 3, Article 68(3), point (*a*)(ii),
of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 covers the applicant subject to the border procedure; once that is
disapplied, the exception does not apply, “with the consequent **revival of the general rule**” of
automatic suspension. It is the same mechanism applied by
[Trib. Torino, 11 August](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-torino-2026-08-11.html), starting from the repeal
of the former Article 28-*bis*(2) of Legislative Decree 25/2008.

The Commissione territoriale did not enter an appearance and did not file the brief referred to in
Article 35-*ter* of Legislative Decree 25/2008.
