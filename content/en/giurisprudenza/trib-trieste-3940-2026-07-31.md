The Tribunale di Trieste (judge Andrea D’Alessio) declared automatically suspended the decision by
which the *Commissione territoriale* of Trieste, on 25 June 2026, had rejected as manifestly unfounded
under Article 32(1), point (*b-bis*), of Legislative Decree 25/2008 an application made by a national
and lodged on 22 June at the *Questura* of Gorizia. The decree is significant because it holds
together two steps that the Bologna line keeps separate.

## “Presentation” as “lodging”: the European reading of the term

On Article 17(4) of Decree-Law 100/2026 the Tribunale takes the opposite position to
[Trib. Bologna 13590 of 4 September](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-13590-2026-09-04.html): the term
“presentation” must be interpreted “as corresponding to the concept of ‘lodging’ proper to European
law, so as to avoid a conflict between domestic and European rules”. Lodging, within the meaning of
Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, is a step distinct from and subsequent to the expression of
the wish (Article 26) and to registration (Article 27); in the present case it coincides with the
drawing up of the C3 form, “that is, 22/6/2026”. Lodging after 12 June entails application of the
new procedure of Article 35-*bis* of Legislative Decree 25/2008 as rewritten by Decree-Law 100/2026.

## The suspensive exception operates only where the accelerated procedure was applied

The decisive step is the following. The new Article 35-*bis*(3) derogates from the rule of the
automatic suspensive effect of the appeal in the cases provided for by Article 68(3) of Regulation
(EU) 2024/1348 — which requires, among other things, that “at the time of the decision” the
Commissione had subjected the applicant to accelerated examination under Article 42(1) or (3). But in
the present case the Commissione relied on the former rules (Article 32(1), point (*b-bis*), of
Legislative Decree 25/2008) and not on the European accelerated procedure: since the procedure
referred to by the provision of the Regulation was not applied, the exception does not apply and the
general rule of automatic suspension for the entire duration of the proceedings resumes force.

The Tribunale does not even decide on the interim request — “consequently, there is no need to
rule” — and closes it: suspension is already in place *ex lege*. The case shows how, even adopting the
most “European” reading of the transitional regime, the Commissione’s rejection remains suspended
where, while characterising the application as manifestly unfounded, it did not actually institute the
accelerated procedure of Article 42 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348.
