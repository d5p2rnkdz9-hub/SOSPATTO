The Tribunal of Trieste (judge Michela Bortolami) suspends the enforceability of the
measure of the Prefect of Trieste of 30 July 2026 — imposing an obligation to reside at the
CAS “Casa Malala” in Fernetti for twelve weeks, pursuant to Article 5-*ter*(2) of Legislative Decree
142/2015 — and orders the immediate interruption of its implementation. The order is made under
Article 700 of the Code of Civil Procedure, during a complaint under Article 5-*quinquies*, in respect of an Afghan national.

## The admissibility of the atypical interim measure

In the absence of a typical interim instrument suited to suspending the prefectural measure in the
period between the filing of the complaint and the decision, Article 700 of the Code of Civil Procedure is “the only possible
interpretative outcome” to avoid the *vulnus* to the rights of defence (Articles 24 and 113
of the Constitution) and to the right to an effective remedy (Article 47 of the EU Charter). The approach is aligned with the
*inaudita altera parte* decrees of the Tribunal of Palermo of 29 June and of Bologna of 10 July 2026,
as well as with Trib. [Genova 9094](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-genova-9094-2026-08-11.html).

## Incidental review of the President's determination

Although not independently challengeable, the determination of the President of the Commission — ordering
the accelerated border procedure — may be reviewed by the court hearing the complaint *incidenter tantum*:
otherwise “the role of review would be emptied of content” over the downstream act, which is a “consequential act
that is bound to follow” (referring to Trib. Bologna, order of 23 July 2026, and to the CJEU, *Samba Diouf*, C-69/10). In
the present case the Prefettura did not enter an appearance and did not produce the determination
TS02I8R767/30.07.2026 referred to in the contested act: where the applicant alleges that he
never received it, the burden of documenting its premises lies with the determining authority.

## The reading of Article 43(1), point (b): “apprehension” requires a close connection

From the documents “it clearly emerges” that there was no apprehension “following
search activity or border control, nor any temporal and spatial connection with the crossing of an
external border”: a condition required by Article 43(1), point (*b*), of Regulation
(EU) 2024/1348 and to which the legal fiction of non-entry under Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1356 is tied,
which “applies only where there is a particularly close temporal and spatial connection”
with the crossing. Ministerial Decree of 5 August 2019 relates to a different procedure; “Ministerial Decree of 21 July 2026, although it entered into force after the present
case, has not resolved this difficulty” of the precise delineation of the border and
transit places.

## Obligation to reside and detention

The authorisation to reside under Article 5-*ter* of Legislative Decree 142/2015 cannot be accompanied by an
enforcement regime comparable to detention: Article 5-*quater*(3) indicates breach of the
measures of the former as a possible premise for the adoption of the latter, and not the reverse.
The absence of typified instruments of judicial control over the actual implementation of the measure
“reinforces” the admissibility of Article 700 of the Code of Civil Procedure, with a ruling putting an end to the treatment
actually imposed.

The order is linked to the line of case law on “apprehension” under Article 43(1), point (*b*) —
[Trib. Milano 36319](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-milano-36319-2026-09-19.html) — and to the incidental review
of the presidential determination in Trib. Bologna of 23 July 2026.
