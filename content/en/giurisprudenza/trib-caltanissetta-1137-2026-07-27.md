The Tribunal of Caltanissetta, Holiday Section — immigration (judge Alice Muratore), in the appeal under Article
35-*ter* of Legislative Decree 25/2008 — having regard to Articles 35-*bis* and 35-*ter* «as amended by Decree-Law of 12
June 2026, no. 100» — has **authorised a national of Bangladesh to remain on the national territory** pending the
conclusion of the proceedings, in effect suspending the effects of the decree of the *Commissione territoriale* of
**Syracuse** which had rejected the application declaring it manifestly unfounded under Article 28-*ter* of Legislative
Decree 25/2008 and Article 42(1), point (b), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, with a simultaneous attestation of the
obligation to return and an entry ban under Article 10(2-*ter*), (2-*sexies*) and (6-*bis*) of Legislative Decree
286/1998.

The appeal alleged breach of the language safeguards and of the right to information, the **excessive curtailment of
the right of defence** resulting from the shortness of the time elapsed between the making of the application and the
audition, the **failure to video-record** the personal interview and the erroneous credibility assessment, which had not
taken into account the applicant's personal context of vulnerability (attacked with a bladed weapon by a client,
threatened with death, with an assault on his sister and a complaint attempted without result).

The Tribunal finds the *fumus boni iuris* established, with reasoning centred on the quality of the interview. The fact
that the applicant had admitted that fellow countrymen had urged him to «tell a story» in order to avoid return is not in
itself capable of grounding a finding of overall lack of credibility, having to be assessed in the context of the other
statements and of his personal circumstances: «although the applicant's account is not free from intrinsic
inconsistencies, the precarious condition in which he found himself at the time of the interview (deriving, *in
primis*, from the excessive curtailment of the right of defence) may have affected the tenor of his statements». In
particular, «the psychological conditioning after the journey and after the forced stay in Libya — certainly not yet
subsided at the time of the interview with the Commission — as well as the **absence of counsel and adequate information
support** resulted in a lack of knowledge of a complete and exhaustive picture of the applicant's condition, on which
the Commission should have based its decision». The failure to video-record the interview, the decree adds, could make a
subsequent audition necessary.

The *periculum in mora* coincides with the concrete and present danger to the applicant's personal safety, who, absent
an authorisation to remain, would be immediately subject to enforcement of the return to Bangladesh, where effective
State protection against the threats suffered might not be guaranteed to him. The hearing for the decision on the merits
will be set by separate order.
