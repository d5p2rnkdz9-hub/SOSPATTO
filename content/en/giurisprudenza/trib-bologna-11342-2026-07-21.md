The Tribunal of Bologna (judge Marco Gattuso) decides what the decree itself calls «the
first request for authorisation to remain brought before this Tribunal in the context of a subsequent
application under the new legal framework», setting out — with the caveat that «every conclusion is
necessarily provisional» — the safeguards that precede a declaration of inadmissibility. A
Senegalese national, who had been granted refugee status in 2017 on account of the risk of
persecution linked to his homosexuality (later reversed on appeal), lodges the application again on 26
June 2026 while detained in a CPR; the Commission declares it inadmissible the same day.

## Legal counselling as a service provided by a person

Articles 15 and 16 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 guarantee the applicant an interview with a legal counsellor at
every stage of the procedure. The obligation cannot be discharged by handing over information material:

> it seems excluded that such legal counselling can be provided by handing out leaflets and
> without interviews with a counsellor, since the Regulation on several occasions refers to a «person»
> who provides the advice […].

The novelty is read as «direct implementation of the right of defence under Article 24 of the Constitution», extended to the
administrative phase. The exception in Article 16(3), which allows Member States to exclude free advice
for abusive subsequent applications, «does not appear to have been transposed into the Italian legal system, so that the
individual right to legal counselling in Italy admits of no exceptions», unless the applicant already has a
lawyer of his own choosing.

## The interview in the preliminary examination and the requirement of «clarity»

Article 55(4) requires the interview also in the preliminary examination, unless it is «clear» from the written submission
that the application involves no new elements.

> the grounds of inadmissibility of the subsequent application (lack of new, relevant
> elements likely to increase the likelihood of a positive determination […])
> must indeed be clearly distinguished from the specific requirement that makes it possible to avoid the mandatory interview preceding that
> inadmissibility, which must be based on the «clarity» of the absence of new elements.

The contested decision had done the opposite, considering the interview superfluous *because* the application
appeared inadmissible. The two obligations are moreover linked: the exception to the interview presupposes that
legal counselling has been provided, because only an informed applicant can knowingly
omit to put forward new grounds.

## Ex officio review and burden of proof

Verification of compliance with the special procedure «in all its stages» is carried out ex officio (Cass.,
Joint Chambers, No. 11399/2024; Cass. No. 6745/2021), and failing this the exception to the right to
remain does not apply. The burden of alleging and proving regularity lies with the determining authority, which is required to give reasons for
the inadmissibility «attesting that the asylum seeker received legal counselling, stating the
name of the counsellor and the date of the interview», and that the personal interview was held
or that it is «clear» that the applicant, informed of the consequences, knowingly omitted to
put forward new grounds. The defect is not cured by characterising the decision as a decision on the merits.

The Tribunal establishes the right to remain for the whole duration of the proceedings; the urgency
connected with the detention «will be assessed by this tribunal by examining the application as a priority».
