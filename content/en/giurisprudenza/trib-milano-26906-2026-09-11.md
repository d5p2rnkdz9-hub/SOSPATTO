The Tribunal of Milan (judge Francesca Laura Stoppa) declares suspended by operation of law the enforceability of the
rejection for manifest unfoundedness issued by the Milan/Monza Commission against a homosexual Bangladeshi national. The
premises are two, systemic in nature: identifying the rules applicable *ratione temporis* and verifying the scope of the
national court's residual review of the designation of a safe country in the passage from Directive 2013/32/EU to the
Procedures Regulation.

## The law applicable *ratione temporis*

The application was lodged on 28 May 2026, the Commission's decision dates from 5 June. Article 79(3) of Regulation (EU)
2024/1348 reserves the new rules for applications lodged from 12 June 2026, so that in the present case Directive
2013/32/EU remains applicable. The decree draws the consequence for Regulation (EU) 2026/464 — the revision of the «safe
country concepts» which, among other things, would limit the court's review to the applicant's individual situation and
prohibit disapplication of the designation:

> having regard to the fact that the operation of the aforementioned Regulation (EU) 2026/464, which in its very title
> states the purpose of amending Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, is manifestly linked to the entry into force of the latter,
> which under Article 79(2) «applies from 12 June 2026»; considering that the right to proper information on the
> procedure adopted must be guaranteed at every stage of the proceedings (CJEU, 30 November 2023, joined cases C-228/21,
> C-254/21, C-297/21 and C-315/21); holding therefore that, also in application of the principle of legal certainty and in
> compliance with the adversarial principle and the right of defence, the new Procedures Regulation cannot apply
> retroactively.

On this point the ruling delivers a principle that the defence can rely on in every case: where the application was
lodged before 12 June 2026, the restrictive regime of review introduced by Regulation (EU) 2026/464 cannot be invoked.

## Disapplication of the designation of Bangladesh as a safe country

On the merits the decree applies the established CJEU framework: judgment of 4 October 2024, C-406/22 (Article 37 precludes
a designation with exceptions for parts of the territory) and, above all, judgment of 1 August 2025, joined cases C-758/24
and C-759/24, which extends the same conclusion to exceptions for categories of persons. The factual premise is
documentary: the MAECI country sheet annexed to the Ministerial Decree of 7 May 2024 that designated Bangladesh — later
transposed into Article 2-bis of Legislative Decree 25/2008 as amended by Decree-Law 158/2024 — «excludes from the safety
assessment persons of non-heterosexual orientation, victims of gender-based violence (including women subjected to female
genital mutilation), ethnic and religious minorities, persons accused of crimes of a political nature and persons
sentenced to death». The update report of 1 April 2025 confirms the picture: discriminatory laws, criminalisation of
homosexual acts (Article 377 of the Bangladeshi Penal Code), police violence and harassment. Hence the disapplication of
Article 2-bis in so far as it designates Bangladesh as a safe country.

## The procedural outcome

With the designation gone, point (c) of paragraph 2 of Article 28-bis, on which the Commission had based the acceleration,
falls away: the ordinary procedure is restored, with suspension of the measure by operation of law and re-expansion to
thirty days of the time limit for appeal. The decision is to be read together with the others published on this site that
disapply the safe-country designation for excluded categories, and with the summary on Trib. Napoli 17573/2026, which set
out the same reasoning for Bangladesh under the new regime (Article 42(1), point (j), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348): two
parallel readings — one under the directive, the other under the regulation — converging on the same outcome, when the
Commission treats the applicant as a «national of a safe country» without probing the category to which he belongs.
