Ten days after the entry into force of Decree-Law 100/2026, the Tribunal of Bologna (judge Chiara Zito) disposes of a
request for validation of detention without touching the merits: the competent judge is no longer that of the place
where the Questore has his seat, but that of the place where the centre is located.

## The facts

A Chilean national is served on 17 June 2026 with the expulsion decree of the Prefect of Bologna and the order for
immediate escort to the border. On 19 June he makes an application for international protection and, on the same day,
the Questore of Bologna orders his detention under Article 6(2)(c) and (d) of Legislative Decree 142/2015 at the CPR of
Bari-Palese. The request for validation reaches the Tribunal of Bologna in good time, within 48 hours.

## The new rule of territorial jurisdiction

The Tribunal raises of its own motion its lack of jurisdiction in favour of the specialised section of the Tribunal of
Bari, on the basis of two insertions made by Decree-Law 100/2026 into Decree-Law 13/2017: Article 14(1)(a), no. 1, added
to Article 3(1) **point (c-bis)**, which assigns to the specialised sections the proceedings for validating the
detention of an applicant for international protection; Article 14(1)(b) **repealed Article 5-*bis***, which provided for
the jurisdiction of the court of appeal «in whose district the Questore who adopted the measure subject to validation
has his seat».

With the special rule gone, the general rule of Article 4(3) of Decree-Law 13/2017 remains: for appellants detained or
accommodated in a centre, the specialised section of the place where the centre is located is competent. To the
objection that that paragraph speaks of *appellants*, whereas in validation the detainee does not appeal, the decree
answers that

> the reference to the word «appellants» contained in paragraph 3 of Article 4 is to be interpreted as a linguistic
> imprecision, the general scope of the paragraph being beyond question since, unlike paragraphs 2 and 2-bis, it does not
> delimit its own field of application, being conversely applicable, by express provision, to (all) the cases in
> paragraph 1 and, therefore, also to the newly introduced point (c-bis).

## Lack of jurisdiction sweeps away the detention

The judge does not merely declare a lack of jurisdiction and remit the file: he regards the point as «dispositive of the
aspects going to the merits» and accordingly does not validate the measure, ordering immediate release unless the person
is detained on another ground. Validation must take place within the mandatory time limit of 48 hours by a competent
judge, and a request addressed to the wrong office does not suspend that time limit.

On the allocation between specialised sections and the Justice of the Peace see
[Cass. n. 24431/2026](/en/giurisprudenza/cass-24431-2026-08-04.html); this decision adds the territorial piece, that is,
*which* specialised section.
