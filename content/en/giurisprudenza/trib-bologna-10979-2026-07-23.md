By its order of 23 July 2026 the Tribunal of Bologna, International Protection Division
(judge Marco Gattuso), decides on the merits the complaint under Article 5-*quinquies* of Legislative Decree
142/2015 already decided on an interim basis by the [decree of 10 July
2026](/en/giurisprudenza/trib-bologna-10979-2026-07-10.html), **upholding the complaint and
annulling** the decision of the Prefect of Bologna that authorised a Pakistani asylum seeker
to «reside only» in a reception centre for a maximum period of
twelve weeks.

As to the **content of the constraint**, the Tribunal rejects the request for a preliminary reference
to the Court of Cassation under Article 363-*bis* of the Code of Civil Procedure (inadmissible in the absence of divergent
interpretative approaches) and clarifies that, since any limitation of freedom must be
expressed in a «manifest, exhaustive and comprehensible» manner, the «authorisation to reside» is
limited to the requirements indicated in writing in the individual decision — in this
case, the daily signature of the attendance register — any further
restriction as to time or territory being excluded. The constraint therefore concerns freedom of
movement and not personal liberty, although it remains the case that the authorisation does not
amount to an authorisation to enter and stay (Article 43(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348: **legal fiction of non-entry**). The decree also records that the respondent *Prefettura* endorsed this
reading, and observes that the *nomen iuris* of «authorisation» adopted by Decree-Law 100/2026
replaces the «obligation to reside in a specific place» of Article 53(4) of Regulation
(EU) 2024/1348.

The decision is nonetheless based on an **overriding evidentiary ground**: the
*Prefettura* did not file the determination of the *Commissione territoriale* of 8 July 2026
that had ordered the mandatory accelerated procedure at the border — a preliminary act
which, in the absence of specific remedies provided for by Regulation (EU) 2024/1348, must necessarily be
assessed *incidenter tantum* by the civil court, including of its own motion (Cass. No. 6745/2021; Cass.
Joint Chambers No. 11399/2024) — nor the documentation on the assessment of vulnerabilities
(a deficiency that had already led the same Commission to annul the first
determination by way of self-review), nor the Eurostat statistical data on which the application of Article 42(1),
point (j), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 rests. Since the special procedures of Section IV of Chapter III of the regulation are
**exceptions** to the ordinary procedure, to be interpreted literally and restrictively,
the burden of alleging and documenting the conditions lies with the respondent Administration and cannot
be shifted onto the complainant by arguing that the documents are held by another
administration (the ex officio route under Article 213 of the Code of Civil Procedure being also excluded).

The Tribunal adds, *for completeness*, that the record of the hearing of 13 July 2026
showed an allegation of the applicant's homosexual orientation, a category for which
the 20% figure cannot be regarded as representative, and recalls that under Articles 53(2)
and 44(1), point (b), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 the border procedure must cease at
any stage at which the conditions are no longer met, with the consequent authorisation to enter
the territory and application of the ordinary procedure.
